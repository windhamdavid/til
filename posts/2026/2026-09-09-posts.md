Today I learned that I actually do want a [flip phone](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)[^1] that costs two thousand dollars. 

<!-- truncate -->

I know... it's absurd but it's also literally my lifeline to family, work, and friends. And I think it's become a lifeline for most folks. My parents in their seventies are rarely more than three feet from their phones. I try to leave it on my desk when I go outside but inevitably end up wanting to photograph my dog or a plant to identify. I might be a [late adopter](https://davidwindham.com/late-adopter/)[^2], but I'm rarely a luddite. Half the reason I'm able to spend so much time outdoors is because of my phone and watch... "hold on a sec while I get back to my desk".

I just finished watching the Apple Announcement and they're pretty good at whipping up some buzz. Regardless, the half shape fold — it opens out into something the size of a small tablet — just makes complete sense especially since they've redesigned how a large chunk of the default apps work within it. It's a large middle-ground size, so I won't be traveling with the iPad next go-round because it's just one more thing to keep up with and the duo screen is just big enough to read a book, play a game, or watch a movie.

Didn't think I'd ever be able to make a stack of phones that costs as much as a base model Toyota. I wrote this essay once about how I used to sell mattresses at a furniture store in college and how the pitch was always you sleep more than anything else you do. I'm quite confident your phone is right up there too in its [Value of Time](https://davidwindham.com/the-value-of-time/)[^3]. 

I'm willing to make some sacrifices though. I don't need a MacBook Pro anymore. I think the M1 will be the last. I'd rather have a small 13" Air if I need to work from somewhere else. I certainly don't need an iPad upgrade when I mostly use it to paint from and control a drone even though I was hyped at the time about the move to the [ARM infrastructure](https://davidwindham.com/arm/)[^4]. And most importantly, I can just lease a 200+ GB RAM GPU machine by the hour instead of the upcoming 20k Mac Studio for the AI work that only takes up a fraction of my desktop usage. If anything, I should have just doubled down on Apple in my [portfolio](/notes/work/stocks)[^5]... I say that everytime.

I interviewed with a crew of Apple folks one time in a hotel suite overlooking the Charleston harbour. I asked what they kept looking at on their phones and one of em laughed and said Apple stock. 

All first world problems... I'm very much aware. Luckily my work demands that I have the devices to test on so it's coming off of that bottom line. It does look like fun to design a dual page app specifically for the forward and reverse screens. I committed to mobile with the first version of an iPhone and that created a serious bump in my career so I'm still kinda nostalgic about it. And we're a team, so that means two of em. I think the only way I can morally justify it is if we do some sort of public community service over the holidays and recycle the old ones.

Truth is... the novelty will wear thin quickly and I've got a perfectly functioning phone. I just like the idea that I could get one and we likely won't until one of em bites the dust or slowly becomes unusable.

I'll give em credit though, they've mastered the product hype introduction events. They also time em ever so perfectly with consumer habits. I like to say "[Designed in Cali--for--nia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCer2e0t8r8)"[^6] like the SNL bit. I think the iPhone will certainly be remembered as the arbiter of the information age much more than any other device. And if Siri would just pick up the slack a bit, we'd be cooking.
