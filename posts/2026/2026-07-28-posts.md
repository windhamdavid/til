Today I learned, or I should say surmised, that internet literacy competence has actually atrophied as folks use it more often. I'm not exactly sure why so this essay is just an exploration on the fact that everyone seems to assume web literacy is universal because it's now so ubiquitous in our lives. 

<!-- truncate -->

I came up with this while sitting in a room for a meeting with other folks where I'm the web guy. I like to try and do my best to listen and not run my mouth when I'm in a room of other folks. I've kinda adopted the it's better to keep your mouth shut than make yourself the fool approach to meetings. 

This idea has kinda been sitting in the back of my mind for at least fifteen years, but it didn't flower until last week. There was a project called ApeNet started in the early 2000s that was an attempt to connect connect great apes over the internet and they provided internet enabled communications for a handful of great apes that were also learning language. I used to reference it regularly regarding out ability to adapt to new forms of communication as it related to the internet.  

I'd previously framed this discussion within technical specifications about how we moved from navigating to receiving information from the internet while moving from open standards to walled gardens. The questions that keep keep getting me in those type of meetings aren't about code, website performance, or servers - they seem to revolve around how we use the internet itself. I think folks just tend to file it under technical expertise. Internet competence and literacy is so broad and full of gaps like we're still figuring it out.  

I tend to lean on evidence like analytics for information about usability. An irony in my professional career has been that I actually prefer to avoid discussions on design even though I have a background in design. It tends to be opinions informed by the same sort of literacy gap that it's easier to just focus on technical execution. I've also learned that a website will oftentimes create all kinds of additional internal questions about the structure and operations of a company or organization eventually leading to said meeting of folks asking themselves, how should we use the internet. 

It's an an evolving gap... just remember that YouTube launched as a video dating site and Shopify sold snowboards. Microsoft was selling baby clothes online in 2000.

![](/img/internet-literacy.jpg)
<div style={{display: 'flex',  justifyContent:'center', alignItems:'center', fontSize:'small', marginBottom:'20px'}}>Microsoft Website - circa 2000</div>

Most people seem to just want instructions and direction. The internet today illustrates that perfectly with everything being pushed as notifications - the red notification bubbles next to the apps on your phone or the text message reminders about whatever. The AI assistants and bots won't just be answering your questions, they will also be messaging you instructions.

I think we can now acknowledge that the leap into the information age is now exposing some elements more akin to sci-fi tech dystopian novels. Some folks have tossed out the civility in communications their grandmas tried to teach em and the public influence enabled by a world wide network are reshaping culture quickly.

I was kinda looking forward to us settling into the internet as a medium and mastering it but instead it keeps evolving so quickly that it's almost a race to whack-a-mole race to keep up. We haven't had time to stop and reflect on what it may or may not be good for. And I think this is exactly where we are now with AI as we're still kinda trying to figure out fundamental questions about the internet and the information age.
